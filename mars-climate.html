Mars Environmental Conditions · Habitability Research

An independent comparison of NASA-published environmental reference measurements, linked to my original paper on future human life on Mars.

What did I find?

NASA reports a mean Mars surface temperature of −65°C versus +15°C for Earth: a difference of 80°C. NASA describes Martian surface pressure as roughly 6–10 millibars, or approximately 0.59%–0.99% of standard Earth sea-level pressure (1,013.25 mbar).

These conditions support my paper's argument that human habitation would require protective shelters and engineered life-support systems. They do not establish whether settlement is feasible.

Method & limitations

I compared published NASA reference figures and computed pressure ratios against a standard Earth sea-level reference. These are global/reference values, not a rover daily weather dataset, and do not measure radiation, water access, or life support performance.

Research question

How do Martian temperature and atmospheric pressure affect exploration?

Scientific background

NASA reports a mean temperature near minus 59 degrees Celsius and mean surface pressure near 6.36 millibars.

Method

I compared published reference values and visualized the large difference between Earth and Mars.

Interpretation

The cold surface and thin carbon dioxide atmosphere create major challenges for spacecraft and human explorers.

Limitations

These are reference comparisons rather than a time series of rover measurements.
